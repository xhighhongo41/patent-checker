# Patent Checker — Important Notice and Consent (version 1)

Read this notice carefully before running a prior-art exploration with
Patent Checker. Your consent is recorded locally together with the date and
the version number of this notice. If the notice changes, you will be asked
to consent again.

## 1. This tool does not make legal judgments

Patent Checker searches public patent data and records observations about
how your project's features relate to the claims of published patent
documents. It never concludes whether anything infringes or does not
infringe, and its reports contain no such verdict. Reading a report as an
infringement opinion is a misuse of this tool.

## 2. Knowledge gained here can be used against you (willful infringement)

Reports and records produced by this tool document that you were aware of
specific patents on specific dates. In some jurisdictions — notably the
United States — evidence that you knew of a patent can support a claim of
willful infringement and increased damages. Conversely, dated records
showing that you reviewed a patent and how you responded (for example, a
design change, or a documented reason why a claim element is not present)
can work in your favor. Patent Checker therefore lets you choose where
results are stored and whether they are tracked in version control; make
that choice deliberately.

## 3. Search results are incomplete

No patent search is exhaustive. Coverage varies by country, language,
publication stage, and data-source availability, and recently published
applications may be missing entirely. The absence of a document from a
report is never evidence that no relevant patent exists.

## 4. This is not professional advice

Patent Checker is a search aid. It is not a substitute for advice from a
qualified patent attorney or agent. For any decision with legal or
commercial consequences, consult a professional.
